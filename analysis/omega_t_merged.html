Does the 1d→2d gain grow when we merge instruments?

Usually, sometimes by a lot, and not because the battery got longer. Across eight DOI families — instruments one cohort actually answered, stacked into a single calibration — the held-out IMV(2PL, 2F-2PL) on the merged battery runs from −0.014 to +0.049, median +0.014. Five of the eight clear the Psychometrika paper's 60-dataset mean of 0.0031 and two clear its maximum of 0.029. In seven of eight the merged battery beats the median of its own member instruments fitted alone. But two families refuse: one is negative merged, and one gains in every instrument separately and nothing at all pooled. And the gain runs against battery size — corr(IMV, n items) = −0.21, where the paper found +0.31 within single tables.

Eight families

Each family is a set of IRW tables sharing a citation key, answered by the same cohort, with no item overlap — one battery a respondent really sat. The merged battery is calibrated on all its items at once; each member instrument is also calibrated alone on its own items, same folds, as the control for test length. Both numbers are the paper's estimand: all held-out responses pooled, one IMV per fold, four folds.

k is member tables in the battery; "members alone" summarises only the members with ≥8 items, which is how many were fitted separately. Graded log score is the held-out mean log score per response, 2d minus 1d, in nats, over the merged battery. colombia is capped at 3,000 of its 46,392 respondents and is 64/73 binary items, so it is the closest of the eight to the published setting.

The two that refuse

chakraborty2026_* — merging destroys the gain

Seven instruments, 140 items, N = 634. Fitted alone, six of seven gain from a second factor: BIDR +0.034, BES +0.023, BSPWBA +0.018, PTM +0.015, IRI +0.013. Merged, the battery is +0.0002. Whatever second dimension each instrument has on its own does not survive being pooled with the others — the 2d solution on 140 items finds something else, and inside it only BES (+0.019 nats) and BIDR (+0.012) improve while the anchor IWAH loses 0.022.

shu_2025_translation_* — actively worse

Six instruments, 45 items, N = 1,054. Merged IMV −0.0135, and the merged graded fit loses 0.15 nats per response — eib −0.354, pcd −0.206, with only pgv (5 items) gaining. All four folds converged, so this is not a fitting failure. A second factor can be straightforwardly harmful out of sample even on six pooled instruments.

The worked example: singh_2025_identity

The first family was taken apart in detail before the other seven were run, and the rest of this page is that dissection. Its merged IMV of +0.0212 is the median of the eight, so it is a fair representative — but the mechanism it shows is not the only one available, as chakraborty above makes clear.

The battery

  • irw_merge("singh_2025_identity_gi") found the family by DOI, confirmed all nine tables share N = 1470, and returned 111,720 rows with a source_table column.
  • 76 items, complete matrix, 5 categories everywhere except _pds at 6.
  • Parallel analysis on this battery suggests 13 factors; the first factor carries 21% of item-correlation variance, against 60%+ for a typical single IRW table.

The protocol

  • 4-fold split over person–item cells, as in the Psychometrika paper's 03_mirt.R. Both models see only in-sample cells, and in the family fit both are calibrated on all 76 items at once — 8 fits in all. A per-item ωt is an evaluation split of those battery-wide fits, never a per-item calibration.
  • mirt graded, 1 factor vs 2 factors; held-out category probabilities from probtrace() on in-sample fscores(), taken unrotated — the default oblimin rotation would pair scores with the wrong slopes.
  • ωt per item, exactly the collapse.qmd definition: binary IMV at each threshold on cumulative probabilities, averaged with category-prevalence weights. ωc computed alongside and agrees.
  • A category needs ≥2% and ≥20 responses or it folds into its neighbour. 33 of 76 items lost one.

Alone, then inside the family

The paper-comparable number

Two estimands were in play here and they are not interchangeable. Domingue et al. (2024) compute one IMV per dataset, over all held-out responses pooled. Everything above averages per-item values. Because IMV's coin transform is nonlinear, averaging it over items weights the items that gain more heavily than the items that lose — so a battery can show a positive per-item average while its total held-out likelihood barely moves. pss responses are predicted 16% better on the likelihood scale and pba's 24% worse, and across the whole battery the graded models net to −0.002 nats. The gain is real and large for the items aligned with the general factor; it is paid for by the items that define the second factor. One consequence worth carrying: the dichotomized 2-factor model gains +0.022 nats per response pooled while the graded one loses 0.002 — the median split discards precisely the information where the graded 2d model mispredicts pba's categories, whose graded loss falls from −0.269 to −0.087 once dichotomized. ωt and the binary IMV are not interchangeable summaries of the same thing.

The second factor is one instrument

Refitting 2 factors on the full matrix and rotating (oblimin, factor correlation 0.15): F2 is essentially pba — mean loading 0.93, with pia at 0.72 and pit at −0.59 — while F1 collects pss, pds, gi and grit. Across items, ωt correlates +0.86 with |F1| loading and −0.71 with |F2|.

So the items that benefit are the ones outside the second cluster. The reading that fits: letting pba's variance leave the general factor sharpens θ for everything else, while pba's own items get worse because their factor is now estimated from 11 items instead of 76. That is a hypothesis, not a result — the 2d→3d step tests it directly.

What this does and doesn't establish

  • ωt's weights are the collapse.qmd ones. Thresholds are weighted by raw category prevalence renormalised over the first K−1. With K ranging 4–6 across items here (after collapsing), that weighting does uneven work. A version exchangeable across K would change these numbers; swapping it is one line and a 20-minute rerun.
  • Per-item averages are not the paper's estimand. The +0.024 above is a mean of per-item ωt; the paper's number is one pooled IMV per dataset. Report the pooled value as the headline and the per-item distribution as the mechanism, never the average of per-item IMVs against a published pooled mean.
  • Eight families, one pair of models. 2d is far short of the 13 factors parallel analysis suggests for singh, and the ladder was not run — so nothing here says where the gain saturates, only that the first extra dimension is worth between −0.014 and +0.049.
  • The gain does not track size, which is the opposite of what a "more items, more dimensions" story predicts: corr(merged IMV, n items) = −0.21, with N = −0.12, with k instruments = +0.29, against the paper's +0.31 with item count within single tables. Eight families is too few to lean on those numbers, but they do not support the simple reading.
  • Two of the eight contradict the headline. The median is a summary of a set that includes a zero and a negative; this is not a monotone effect of merging.
  • The binary arm required a median split, with base rates from 0.10 to 0.50. In 13 of 304 item-folds one or both models scored below −log(2) and imv.binary floored the coin at 0.5; those item-folds contribute a 0 rather than a negative value.
  • The second factor is a single instrument, so this is as between-item as multidimensionality gets — the easiest case for a 2-factor model, exactly the case the paper's SI predicted would pay most.
  • 33 of 76 items had a category collapsed. Not a defect, but the graded structure is thinner than a nominal 5-point scale suggests.

What went wrong building the batteries

Three traps cost real time, and the first two change results silently rather than erroring.

  • irw_merge() drops members whose column set differs from the first table's, with a message() as the only signal and a plausible-looking result returned. It builds the battery by incremental rbind, so IRW tables carrying different cov_* columns fail to bind and are skipped; which members survive depends on fetch order. act_kay_2025 came back with 5 of 12 members and 20 of 88 items; chakraborty2026_IWAH_IRW lost all six non-anchor tables and returned NULL. Filed as itemresponsewarehouse/Rpkg#224. The batteries here were assembled instead by fetching each member directly and keeping only id/item/resp, with item names prefixed by table so two members cannot collide.
  • A median split can leave a constant column and kill the 2PL fit outright. On a skewed or binary item the median sits at a category boundary, every response falls on one side, and mirt stops with "only one response category". 7 of 140 items in chakraborty and 51 of 73 in colombia — which is 64/73 binary — so both families failed every fold on the first pass. The dichotomiser now falls back to the most even available cut; no item in singh or the first batch was degenerate, so those numbers are unchanged.
  • The scout cache rots. The vetted group list is from 26 August 2026. All nine uruguay_2022_technology_* tables now return "table does not exist in IRW", and act_kay_2025's tables turned out to carry two rows per person–item cell — repeated measures, out of scope here. Both were dropped and replaced. Any larger sweep should re-run the scout first.

Where this stands

Ben's call, 28 October: stop with the eight families rather than climb the dimension ladder, and hold the write-up rather than place it. So this is the result, not a staging post.

  1. Open: is ωt exchangeable across K? The collapse.qmd definition weights thresholds by raw category prevalence renormalised over the first K−1, and K ranges 3–6 across these families after collapsing. Ben's read is that it is exchangeable; worth confirming with Klint before any of these per-item numbers becomes a figure. The pooled IMV headline does not depend on it.
  2. Not done: the item-count-matched control. 1d-vs-2d on a random subset of the merged matrix the size of one member instrument. The alone-fits already argue against a length effect, and the −0.21 correlation argues against it harder, but this would close it properly.
  3. Not done: the dimension ladder. The interesting target is chakraborty rather than singh — if a second factor helps each instrument separately but not the pool, 7d (one factor per instrument) is the direct test. Deferred.
  4. Not done: a binary arm. About 35 binary families pass a comparable metadata filter, which would put merged batteries on the exact scale of the published benchmark. colombia is accidentally most of the way there at 64/73 binary items and lands at +0.0016.
  5. Held: where it lands. #merged-batteries in dimensionality.qmd is the natural home, but it is not being written up yet.
  6. Carried forward: both estimands, always. Pooled IMV as the headline for comparability, the per-item distribution for mechanism, and the aggregate log score as the check that a positive average is not hiding a zero-sum redistribution. All three disagree somewhere in these eight families.

A note: ENEM would be the clean test

not run · noted 28 October 2026

Every number on this page went through a median split to reach the paper's binary scale, and that step is the one methodological wart in the whole exercise — it is what flipped the aggregate verdict on singh (+0.022 nats dichotomized against −0.002 graded), and it is what broke chakraborty and colombia on the first pass. The 52 enem_* tables remove it: INEP scores the items 0/1 itself, so a merged ENEM battery gives a pooled IMV directly comparable to 0.0031 and 0.029 with nothing in between.

The structure is also right by construction rather than by the identical-N proxy used everywhere above. src/data/enem_2019.R draws one 1M sample of candidates per year — set.seed(5150), from candidates INEP records as present in all four areas — and only then splits that same sample into the four area tables, with id being the candidate's own NU_INSCRICAO. So the four tables for a year are the same million people with disjoint items and density 1.0. Nothing needs verifying; the sampling sits upstream of the split.

A year's battery

  • 185 binary items — 45 each for humanities (ch), natural sciences (cn) and maths (mt), 50 for languages (lc).
  • N ≈ 1,000,000, four instruments, 13 years (2013–2025) — thirteen replications of one design rather than a grab-bag of heterogeneous families.
  • Four curricular areas should be multidimensional, so this is close to a positive control: a null here would say something about the method, not about ENEM.
  • At N = 1M the sample size can be varied freely, which none of the eight families could support — so the gain's dependence on N becomes estimable without confounding.

Three things to handle first

  • irw_merge cannot assemble 9 of the 13 years. Only 2013, 2016, 2018 and 2019 have identical N across all four areas; elsewhere cn and mt lose a few hundred candidates to the off-length TX_RESPOSTAS filter, so the identical-N gate fails. fetch_direct.R already handles it.
  • Scale is the one real design choice. 185 items × 1M people is 185M responses a year, against 47k–178k for everything above. A few thousand candidates per year is the obvious start.
  • lc carries missingness by design. Its 50 items include both alternate language blocks, 5 items each, and every candidate sat only one — hence density 0.9. Fine for mirt as NA, and arguably a feature: a built-in between-item multidimensionality, the case the paper's SI predicts should pay most.

One limit on id: 2024 and 2025 take it from NU_SEQUENCIAL, which the build script's own comment flags as a within-year surrogate carrying no meaning across years. That does not touch merging the four areas within a year, but it rules out stacking years into one person-level battery.
