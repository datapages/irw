Item Text

ImportantAbout these data

IRW item text is reconstructed from published articles, codebooks, and supplementary materials using a largely automated pipeline with partial human review. It is provided for research purposes only. We make no guarantee that the text is accurate, complete, or correctly aligned with the item identifiers in the corresponding IRW response data; the original source document is always authoritative. Users are responsible for verifying item text against that source before drawing substantive conclusions. See Known Issues for documented discrepancies, and please get in touch if you find others.

Note also that the licenses recorded for IRW response data do not extend to the instruments themselves. Inclusion of item text here implies no license to reuse an instrument; copyright and related rights remain with the original rights holders, and users are responsible for obtaining any permissions their intended use requires.

Warningartistic_preferences

The table name describes only the first of three instruments administered in the same session: items 1-30 are the Artistic Preferences Scales, items 31-40 the Ten Item Personality Inventory, and items 41-56 a 16-word vocabulary check list. Items 1-30 have no text stem at all – each is a pair of paintings, shown as images, that the respondent chooses between and rates the strength of the preference; the images are distributed with the source dataset. Warningarzamoncunill_2023_epq_clinical

None of the item text here is the wording respondents read. item_text is the short category descriptor the study’s supplement gives for each item number (“Editable agenda”, “Videoconference”), which names what the item is about rather than quoting it. The study ran a 43-item pretest pool but published full item wording, in Spanish, only for the 26 items kept in its final questionnaire, while this table holds all 22 clinical-care items of the pretest pool – so 10 of the 22 have no published wording in any language, and the descriptor is the only text that exists for them. For the ee_quality item the paper’s own section 4.2 quotes a different wording (“There is a strong focus on growth-oriented and productive entrepreneurship activity in my region (city)”) from the data-file label that is shipped here (“There is a strong entrepreneurship ecosystem (EE) in my region (city)”). Warningbaaziz_2023_sms2

The text shown for item 16 is probably not the item respondents answered, and should not be used as the wording of that item. The paper prints “So that others praise me for what I do” at position 16, which is external-regulation content, but in the data item 16 behaves as amotivation: it correlates 0.88-0.90 with items 17 and 18, the other two amotivation items, and essentially zero (0.02, 0.04, -0.03) with the three external-regulation items 13-15. Position 16 in the SMS-II is an amotivation item, so the paper’s table appears to misprint its wording; the printed text is shipped here unchanged rather than substituted with a guess. Warningbaka2023_bpnsf

The response anchors here are given in the OPPOSITE direction to the study data file’s own value labels, which state 1 = Strongly agree through 7 = Strongly disagree. The data contradict that: scoring the file as labelled would make every need-satisfaction item correlate negatively with the same respondents’ work engagement, measured on an unambiguous 0-6 Never..Always scale. Sorted by item content, all 12 satisfaction items correlate +0.28 to +0.40 with that engagement score and all 12 frustration items -0.03 to -0.22, which is only possible if the scale runs 1 = Strongly disagree to 7 = Strongly agree, as shipped. Item text is the administered Polish, with the source file’s typos retained

Warningbaka2023_olbi

Warningbakker_2020_rses

The item codes for this table must not be read as item content. Seven of the ten columns in the study’s SPSS file carry a variable label that contradicts the column name, and the data show the labels are the reliable half: the column named goodqualities303 is labelled “All in all, I am inclined to feel that I am a failure” and averages 1.47 on a 1-4 Strongly Disagree..Strongly Agree scale, while nogood302 is labelled “I feel that I have a number of good qualities” and averages 3.27. Every negatively-worded label sits at the floor and every positively-worded one at the ceiling, which a positively-worded self-esteem item could not do in a general sample. Read in column order the labels reproduce a standard circulated administration order of the scale, so the names appear to have been assigned by numbering the columns in Rosenberg’s original order without noticing the form was permuted. The text shipped here follows the labels; join on the item code and read the text, not the code. Responses are stored raw and unreversed. Note also that the paper’s Methods states the scale ran 1 = strongly agree to 4 = strongly disagree, the reverse of the data file’s own value labels; the data follow the file

Warningbang_2023_self_esteem

The item order in this table was reconstructed, not read from any key, and the item text is not what respondents saw. Neither the study’s data file (bare codes S1-S10) nor the paper ties any code to an item, so the shipped text is the canonical English Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale wording placed in an order inferred from the reverse-keying pattern in the raw responses; the study administered the Korean version (Jon, 1974), whose wording is not reproduced anywhere. What the data establish is each item’s polarity: S1, S2, S4, S6 and S7 intercorrelate positively (+0.31 to +0.73) and S3, S5, S9 and S10 form the opposing block, which rules out the scale’s original item numbering, and S8 is identifiable by its distinctive near-zero correlation with the positive block. What they do not establish is the order within each polarity block, so a swap between two same-polarity items – S1 and S2, say – is not excluded

Warningbartoli_2022_badge_notifications

This table records a single-click behavioural task rather than a questionnaire. Each participant saw one home screen of 15 app icons with a badge notification on one app and made a single click, so resp = 100 marks the one app that participant clicked first and 0 the other fourteen. The response-option text is descriptive; the task offered no labelled options. The item text gives each app’s conventional name, with the Italian default-app labels left as administered

Warningbeck_2021_iesr

resp values 1-4 are category indices, not scores. The study used the German IES-R (Maercker & Schuetzwohl, 1998), whose four anchors are ueberhaupt nicht / selten / manchmal / oft and whose traditional scoring weights that version prints are 0, 1, 3 and 5 – so summing resp gives a different scale from the published one. Item text is that German form verbatim, including its pre-1996 orthography

Warningbeck_2021_pss10

The PSS-10 was administered in German, but the study names no German translation, so the item text shown here is the original English wording rather than what respondents read. Items 4, 5, 7 and 8 are stored unreversed and must be reversed before scoring, and the response coding is the canonical 0-4 shifted by one (1 = Never … 5 = Very often)

Warningbitew_2020_lte

Item text is the study’s own abbreviated SPSS variable labels (“self illness”, “family death”), not the administered wording, which was in Amharic and was never published. Canonical Life Events Questionnaire wording was deliberately not substituted because this version departs from it: items 8-11 are reordered relative to the original sequence, and item 12 (intimate partner violence) is not an LTE-Q item at all

Warningbitew_2020_osss3

Item wording is the canonical English OSSS-3 rather than the study’s administered Amharic version. One caveat on the mapping: item 1’s variable label in the source file reads “others worry for me”, which does not obviously correspond to the canonical item-1 content about how many people you can count on. It is identified as item 1 by its response structure instead – it is the only one of the three offering four categories rather than five, which is the instrument’s own signature, and the file’s total column equals the sum of the three items for every case

Warningbitew_2020_phq9

Item wording is the canonical English PHQ-9; the study administered a locally validated Amharic translation whose exact wording is not published. The mapping to those canonical items is confirmed by the source file’s own numbered symptom labels and by the paper’s reported per-item means

Warningbitew_2020_self_efficacy

This table holds 9 of the General Self-Efficacy Scale’s 10 items, and its item codes are offset by one: SEFFICAn is GSE item n+1, so SEFFICA1 is GSE item 2. The source file does contain GSE item 1, under a differently-named column, but it was not shipped to the IRW. The response anchors are the authors’ own shorthand for the GSE’s Not at all true…Exactly true scale

Warningbrederecke_2020_sis

Items 4 and 9 are stored reverse-coded in the source data, and the option text here follows that stored direction: for those two items resp = 1 is “strongly agree” and 5 is “strongly disagree”, while the other nine run 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree. This is stated by the study’s own file, which names those two columns “SIS Item 4 recoded” and “SIS Item 9 recoded” – they are the scale’s two negatively-worded items. Item text gives the English wording followed by the German actually administered

Warningbroadband_inventories

The instructions, quoted verbatim from the test’s own page, describe a five-point scale, but the items were administered on the seven-point Strongly disagree..Strongly agree scale that resp 1-7 encodes. The item wording is otherwise verbatim from the source codebook, including its typos (item 18 reads “when Im feeling badly”)

Warningbuczel_2022_inoculation_belief

Each of these six items covers six different question wordings. The study counterbalanced six scenarios across participants, so the sentence any one participant read depends on which scenario they saw; item_text therefore names the item’s condition and quotes one scenario’s wording as an example rather than presenting one participant’s version as the item. Note also that the response scale displayed to participants ran 0-10 while resp is stored as 1-11, so the option text carries the displayed number and is deliberately not equal to resp

Warningbukurov_2022_sf36

Item and option wording is the public-domain English RAND 36-Item Health Survey. The study administered a Serbian-language SF-36, so this is canonical text rather than what participants read. The mapping to those canonical items is firm: the source file labels every column with its item, and the per-item response formats match the instrument’s mixed structure exactly (5 levels for items 1, 2, 20, 22 and 32-36; 3 for items 3-12; 2 for 13-19; 6 for 21 and 23-31)

Warningburkert_2019_whoqol_bref

Items LQ3 (pain), LQ4 (dependence on medical treatment) and LQ26 (negative feelings) are stored already reverse-coded, so for those three resp = 5 is the most favourable answer, and the option text here reflects that stored direction rather than the order printed on the questionnaire – which the source file’s own value labels still describe. The data settle it: those three items correlate positively (+0.18 to +0.46) with unambiguously positive-keyed items such as overall quality of life and mobility, and 52% of respondents sit at LQ3 = 5, which is only interpretable as “not at all hindered by pain” in a general-population sample

Warningbusch_2022_course_alleviate

These are the checkbox options of a single select-all-that-apply survey question, not independently administered items: resp = 1 means the respondent ticked that aspect and 0 means they did not, so a 0 records the absence of a tick rather than substantive disagreement. The question was shown only to the respondents who reported depression symptoms while taking online science courses

Warningbrederecke_2020_phq4

The item text is the study data file’s own terse English variable labels (“little interest or pleasure in your activities”), which are neither the German wording the instrument was administered in nor the canonical PHQ-4 phrasing. The response options are that file’s English labels too, and they likewise differ from the published PHQ-4 anchors – “On single days” where the instrument reads “Several days”. The instructions line is the canonical PHQ-4 stem, which the data file does not carry

Warningburgess_2025_soas

The item codes are non-contiguous – SOAS5, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 12 – because they refer to positions in the 13-item version administered in this study, of which these six were retained in the final scale. The study’s data file carries no variable or value labels, so the item text and that numbering come from the paper’s own item table rather than from the data. In this study the items were read aloud to children who were shown a fixed object, so the published instrument’s opening line asking the respondent to choose and write down an object did not apply and is not included here

Warningbrain_hemisphere

The instructions field describes the response scale rather than quoting an instruction shown to participants; the source dataset documents the scale but publishes no participant-facing instruction text. Only three of the five scale points were labelled (1 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 5 = Agree), so the option text is blank at 2 and 4. Note also that this 2015-16 dataset is the 20-item version of the scale; the version currently offered online has 24 items

Warningbusch_2023_stigma

Despite the table name, these items are not stigma ratings. They are the participants’ own binary self-reports of concealable identities – LGBQ+, depression, anxiety, low SES, first-generation status, academic struggle, disability, community-college transfer – while the study’s actual 4-point perceived-stigma measure is not in the IRW table at all. Six of the eight items share the identical stem “I most closely identify as”, so the item content lives entirely in the response options. For two items the binary 0 collapses several source options (for example mid and upper income both map to 0), and the option text joins those source options verbatim; “Decline to state” responses are dropped rather than coded

Warningbutt_2022_cognitive_absorption

The study’s data file contains four of the six Cognitive Absorption items – CA3 and CA5 were dropped by the authors – and it carries no variable labels for them, so the wording here comes from the appendix questionnaire, which lists the six items in order without codes. The alignment is therefore by position within that block

Warningbutt_2022_institutional_factors

Eleven of the appendix’s thirteen Institutional Factors items are in the data; IF3 and IF11 are absent from the study’s own file and are not shipped. The data file gives no variable labels for these columns, so the wording comes from the appendix questionnaire by position within the block. One source typo was corrected: the appendix prints “students ’questions” with a misplaced opening quote, shipped here as “students’ questions”

Warningbutt_2022_actual_usage

This two-item scale does not use the questionnaire’s 1-7 agreement scale: each item has its own response anchors – a frequency scale for one and hours per week for the other – so instructions is blank because the block’s shared instruction does not apply

Warningbuzgova_2023_rses

The item numbering here is an assumption that could not be independently confirmed. The study’s data file carries no variable or value labels of any kind and the paper reproduces no items, so the text is the canonical English Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale mapped on the basis that RSESn is Rosenberg item n. That assumption is known to fail for this instrument: in bakker_2020_rses the columns were numbered in canonical order while the administered form was permuted. Here the responses are stored already reverse-scored – every inter-item correlation is positive – so the keying-polarity check that caught the other case cannot be run. One position does have positive support: RSES8 has both the lowest mean and the lowest item-rest correlation, the known signature of Rosenberg item 8 (“I wish I could have more respect for myself”). Positions 1-7, 9 and 10 rest on the numbering alone. The scale is also stored pre-reversed, so the same anchors apply to every item

Warningbuzgova_2023_soc

Item text is the canonical English SOC-13; the study was Czech and its translation is not published in the paper or the data file, which carries no labels of any kind. The responses are stored already reverse-scored, so the option text for the five reverse-worded items (1, 2, 3, 7 and 10) is flipped relative to the printed instrument. Note also a discrepancy with the paper: it reports Cronbach’s alpha of 0.92 for this scale, while the released data give 0.765 with corrected item-total correlations of 0.29-0.53

Warningbuzgova_2023_gai

Item text is the canonical English Geriatric Anxiety Inventory; the study was Czech and its translation is not published, and the data file carries no labels of any kind. The mapping of code to item is supported but not settled: the instrument’s published three-factor structure reproduces for 15 of the 20 items, with the three purely somatic items all behaving as expected, while five cognitive-versus-arousal items are ambiguous

Warningbuzgova_2023_gds

Item text is the canonical English GDS-15; the study was Czech and its translation is not published in the paper or the data file, which carries no labels of any kind.
